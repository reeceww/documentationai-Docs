## What Hotline is, from your side

A brand you work with runs their creator programme on Hotline. You upload your videos here, they review them here, and when a video becomes an ad you can see what it did and what it earned you.

The part that is unusual is the last one. Your royalties are calculated from the real numbers on your own ads rather than agreed after the fact, and you can see those numbers yourself.

<Callout kind="info">
  You only ever see your own work. Account wide spend, the brand's ROAS and the other creators on the roster are not visible to you.
</Callout>

## Getting set up

You join from an invitation the brand sends to your email. The invitation shows you the terms before you accept, so you know what you are being paid before you agree to anything.

## Where things are

| Tab | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Home | A summary of your work and earnings |
| Hub | Briefs, My Creatives, Products, Documents and Boards |
| Messages | Your threads with the brand |
| Performance | What your videos did as ads |
| Earnings | What you have earned, and cashing out |
| Settings | Your profile, your payout method, your emails |

## Working from a brief

Brands post briefs in **Hub**, then **Briefs**. A brief says what to make, which angles to try, which hooks to avoid, how many videos are wanted and by when.

Your Briefs tab is in two parts: **Yours**, the briefs you hold, and **Up for grabs**, everything open to you that you have not taken.

Claim one to say you are working on it, or a brand can put you on one directly. If a brief asks for a set number of videos, only that many creators can hold it at once. You can give a brief back if your plans change.

Your submission attaches to the brief you made it for, so both sides can see what has arrived against what was asked for.

<Callout kind="info">
  You only see briefs that are open to you. A brand can post one to everybody, to a segment, or to named creators.
</Callout>

## Uploading

Uploads go in **Hub**, then **My Creatives**. Each one takes a title, an optional note to the brand, and the file.

Every upload is a version, including the first. If the brand asks for changes, your new cut is added as the next version rather than replacing what you sent, and the note that prompted the change stays attached to the take it was written about.

## Review and revisions

The brand watches your video and either approves it or asks for changes. Their notes can be pinned to a specific moment in the video, so feedback points at the frame it is about instead of describing it.

A revision request is not a rejection. Answer it by uploading the new cut, which arrives as the next version with the original still there.

## Requesting product

If the brand has connected Shopify, their catalogue appears in **Hub**, then **Products**. Request what you need to film with, and the brand approves it or sends something over unprompted.

Some brands set limits on how much can be requested at once or how often. Going over one does not block your request, it just means a person looks at it.

Your orders and their tracking sit on the same tab.

## Performance

Once an approved video is running as an ad, its numbers flow back roughly every two hours. **Performance** shows impressions, clicks, click through rate and what each video has earned you.

A video running as several ads can be read either as one card per ad or folded into a single card with a variant count.
